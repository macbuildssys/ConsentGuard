# Best effort statement

ConsentGuard works on a best effort basis.

## The short version

ConsentGuard tries to reject cookie and tracking choices for you and to switch off the optional ones. It can miss some. It cannot promise that a site stops tracking you, and it cannot promise that any law is met. Choices a site marks as required or essential are left on. If ConsentGuard is not sure that every optional switch is off, it does not press Save or Confirm and leaves the panel as it is. Check the sites that matter to you yourself.

## What ConsentGuard tries to do

When it sees a cookie or tracking prompt, it taps a reject option, or a necessary only option, or it opens the settings and switches optional choices off one by one. When it is working through switches, it then presses Save or Confirm if the site asks for that.

## What it does not promise

- That every optional choice on every banner is switched off.
- That a site will stop tracking you, or will respect what was chosen.
- That using it meets the requirements of any privacy law, for you or for a site.
- That it works on every app or website, or keeps working when a site changes its banner.

## Why it can miss something

- Banners are built in many different ways and their wording and layout change without notice.
- Some sites draw their switches in a way Android cannot see, so ConsentGuard cannot read or change them.
- Some panels list hundreds of separate companies. ConsentGuard has limits on how far it scrolls and how often it taps, so a very long panel may not be checked completely.
- A slow page may show its options late, or change them after ConsentGuard has looked.
- A tap can fail to register, and on some banners ConsentGuard cannot see whether a switch really changed.

## What it does when it is not sure

- It does not press Save or Confirm if a switch that should be off is still on, or if the panel was too long to check completely. The panel is left as it is, so you can finish it yourself.
- It is built to skip buttons that read like accept all, pay, subscribe or sign in.

## Where it acts

ConsentGuard only acts on a screen that looks like a cookie or tracking consent banner: consent wording together with buttons a banner has. It does not act in its own app, in the phone settings, in permission and install dialogs, on the home screen, in the phone app or in the keyboard. It cannot be certain. A screen that looks enough like a banner could still be tapped by mistake.

## What it leaves on

Switches marked as required, essential, necessary, mandatory, always on, or similar are left on, because a site may not work without them. ConsentGuard cannot check that such a label is truthful. A label that says required but also names advertising or measurement is not trusted, and that switch is turned off. Switches that protect you, such as "do not sell my data", are also left on.

## What it reads and keeps

ConsentGuard reads the text on the screen to find cookie and tracking prompts. The app has no internet permission and sends nothing anywhere. It keeps nothing except your settings, and a release build writes nothing to the system log.

## Your part

- For sites where it matters, check the cookie settings yourself.
- Report a banner it misses so it can be added.
- Turn the service off in the accessibility settings whenever you like.
